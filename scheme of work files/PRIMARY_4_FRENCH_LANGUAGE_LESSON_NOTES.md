**PRIMARY 4 FRENCH LANGUAGE LESSON NOTES FIRST TERM**

**WEEK 1: POURQUOI APPRENDRE LE FRANÇAIS? (WHY LEARN FRENCH?).**

**OBJECTIVES **By the end of the lesson, pupils should be able to:

- State two reasons why learning French is important.

- List three French-speaking countries in Africa (Francophone countries).

- Identify Nigeria's neighbors that speak French.

- Recite the first 5 letters of the French alphabet.

**CONTENT Importance of Learning French: **French is not just another subject; it is a key that opens many doors. It is an *international language*, which means it is spoken by millions of people in many countries around the world, not just in France.

**Learning French helps us in our daily lives and future:**

- **To Communicate: **Nigeria shares borders with countries where French is the main language. If you learn French, you can talk to your neighbors, make new friends, and understand them when you visit their markets or towns.

- **For Better Opportunities: **When you grow up, knowing French can help you get interesting jobs. You could work as a *translator *(helping people understand each other), in *tourism *(guiding French visitors around Nigeria), or even for big international organizations.

- **For Education and Travel: **Many interesting books, cartoons (like *Asterix*), websites, and news reports are in French. Learning it lets you enjoy them. Also, if you travel to countries like Benin or Niger, you can ask for directions, buy things, and feel more confident.

**French in Nigeria and Africa (Francophone Africa): **A *Francophone country *is a country where French is widely spoken. In Africa, there are many!

**Nigeria's French-Speaking Neighbors: **Look at a map. Nigeria is like a big house with neighbours who speak French. These neighbours are:

- *Bénin *(Benin Republic) - to the West

- *Niger *- to the North

- *Tchad *(Chad) - to the North-East

- *Cameroun *(Cameroon) - to the East

**Other Major Francophone African Countries: **French is spoken in many countries across West and Central Africa. Some big ones are:

- *Sénégal *(Senegal)

- *Côte d'Ivoire *(Ivory Coast)

- *Mali*

- *Togo*

**Basic French Alphabet (Introduction): **The French alphabet has 26 letters, just like English, but the *sounds *are often different. It's like learning a new song for familiar letters. Let's start with the first five. We pronounce them clearly and slowly.

- **A **(ah) - Sounds like the "a" in "father".

- **B **(bay) - Sounds like "bay" without the 'y' sound at the end.

- **C **(say) - Sounds like the English word "say".

- **D **(day) - Sounds like the English word "day".

- **E **(uh) - Sounds like a short "uh" sound, like the 'e' in "the".

**Tip: **Your mouth shape is very important in French. For 'E' (uh), keep your lips relaxed and your jaw slightly open.

**Motivation and Fun: **Learning French is an adventure! You can start by learning simple songs ("Frère Jacques" is a popular one), watching educational cartoons, or learning how to say the names of your favourite Nigerian foods in French. Every new word you learn is a step towards becoming a language expert!

# WEEK 2: SALUER (GREETINGS) - PART 1.

**OBJECTIVES **By the end of the lesson, pupils should be able to:

- Differentiate between formal and informal greetings.

- Say "Good Morning" and "Good Evening" in French.

- Ask "How are you?" in French.

- State three appropriate responses to "How are you?".

**CONTENT Formal vs. Informal Greetings: Knowing the Difference: **Knowing who you are talking to is very important in French, just like in Nigerian culture. We use different greetings to show respect.

**Formal Greetings (Politesse): **We use these with people we respect, people older than us, or people we don't know very well. Examples: your teacher, your principal, your friend's parents, a market stall owner.

- *Bonjour *(bohn-zhoor): This means *"Good day" *or *"Hello"*. It is the most common and polite greeting. You use it from the time you wake up until around *6:00 PM*.

- *Bonsoir *(bohn-swahr): This means *"Good evening"*. You use it from around *6:00 PM*

until you go to bed.

- *Bonne nuit *(bun nwee): This means *"Good night"*. You *only *use this when someone is going to sleep. It is not an evening greeting.

**Informal Greetings (Amiable): **We use these with people of the same age, close friends, or family members (like siblings).

- *Salut *(sah-loo): This is a casual *"Hi" *or *"Bye"*. It's friendly and relaxed.

- *Coucou *(koo-koo): This is a very playful, informal "hello," like "peekaboo" or "hey there!" often used with close friends or young children.

**Asking "How Are You?" (Comment ça va?): **After saying "Bonjour" or "Salut", it is polite to ask about the person's well-being.

- **Formal Question: ***Comment allez-vous? *(koh-mohn tah-lay voo). This is the respectful way to ask an adult or someone important.

- **Informal Question: ***Comment ça va? *(koh-mohn sah vah) or simply *Ça va? *(sah vah). This is what you use with friends and classmates.

**How to Respond (Les Réponses): **When someone asks you "Ça va?", you can reply in many ways:

- *Ça va bien. *(sah vah byehn) - *I am fine.*

- *Très bien. *(treh byehn) - *Very well.*

- *Comme ci, comme ça. *(kohm see, kohm sah) - *So-so. *(You say this while making a flat, side-to-side hand gesture).

- *Oui, ça va. Merci. *(Wee, sah vah. Mair-see) - *Yes, I'm okay. Thank you.*

It is always polite to say *"Merci" *(Thank you) after responding, and you can also ask back: *"Et toi?" *(And you?) to your friend or *"Et vous?" *to an adult.

# WEEK 3: SALUER (GREETINGS) - PART 2.

**OBJECTIVES **By the end of the lesson, pupils should be able to:

- Mention three ways to say goodbye in French.

- Identify "Magic Words" (polite expressions) in French.

- Perform a simple greeting role-play.

- Describe the French custom of "la bise."

**CONTENT Saying Goodbye (Les Au Revoir): **Just like greetings, there are different ways to say goodbye depending on when you will see the person again.

- *Au revoir *(oh ruh-vwahr): This is the standard, polite *"Goodbye"*. You can use it in most situations, formal or informal.

- *À bientôt *(ah byehn-toh): This means *"See you soon"*. Use it when you expect to see the person again in a few days or weeks.

- *À demain *(ah duh-mahn): This means *"See you tomorrow"*. Perfect for your classmates at the end of the school day.

**The "Magic Words" (Les Mots Magiques): **These are short, powerful words that make you sound polite and respectful. They are like magic because they make people happy to help you!

- *S'il vous plaît *(seel voo pleh) - *Please *(formal).

- *S'il te plaît *(seel tuh pleh) - *Please *(informal, to a friend).

- *Merci *(mair-see) - *Thank you*.

- *De rien *(duh ryehn) - *You're welcome *(Literally "It's nothing").

- *Excusez-moi *(ex-koo-zay mwah) - *Excuse me *(to get attention politely).

- *Pardon *(pahr-dohn) - *Sorry *(for a small bump) or *Excuse me *(to get past someone).

- *Désolé(e) *(day-zoh-lay) - *Sorry *(for a mistake. Add 'e' if you are a girl: *Désolée*).

**A Simple Greeting Role-Play: **Let's put it all together in a short conversation between two friends, Kofi and Aisha.

- **Kofi: **Salut, Aisha! (Hi, Aisha!)

- **Aisha: **Salut, Kofi! Ça va? (Hi, Kofi! How are you?)

- **Kofi: **Ça va bien, merci. Et toi? (I'm fine, thank you. And you?)

- **Aisha: **Très bien! Au revoir! (Very well! Goodbye!)

- **Kofi: **À demain! (See you tomorrow!)

**Cultural Note: Greetings in France (La Bise): **In France and some other French-speaking countries, friends and family often greet each other with *"la bise" *(lah beez). This is not a kiss on the lips. It is a light touch of the cheeks while making a soft kissing sound in the air. They usually do it twice (left cheek, then right cheek). It's a sign of friendship.

**In Nigeria: **We don't typically do *la bise*. We shake hands, wave, or give a respectful nod. It's important to know about other cultures, but in your French class, a smile and a "Bonjour" are perfect!

# WEEK 4: SE PRÉSENTER (INTRODUCING ONESELF) - PART 1.

**OBJECTIVES **By the end of the lesson, pupils should be able to:

- Introduce themselves by stating their names in French.

- Ask someone for their name.

- State their nationality in French.

- List the basic personal pronouns (I, You, He, She).

**CONTENT Saying Your Name (Je m'appelle...): **The most important thing to tell someone is your name! In French, there are two main ways:

- *Je m'appelle... *(zhuh mah-pell...): This literally means *"I call myself..."*. It is the most common and natural way to say "My name is..."

- Example: *Je m'appelle Chiamaka. *(My name is Chiamaka.)

- *Je suis... *(zhuh swee...): This means *"I am..."*. It is also correct but is a bit more direct.

- Example: *Je suis Ahmed. *(I am Ahmed.)

**Asking Someone's Name: **To make a new friend, you need to ask their name. Remember the difference between formal ("vous") and informal ("tu").

- **Informal (to a classmate or child): ***Comment tu t'appelles? *(koh-mohn too tah-pell?) - What is your name?

- **Formal (to a teacher or adult): ***Comment vous appelez-vous? *(koh-mohn voo zah-play voo?) - What is your name?

**Stating Your Nationality (Être Nigérian(e)): **To say where you are from, we use *"Je suis" *(I am) + your nationality. In French, the nationality word changes for boys and girls.

- I am Nigerian (a boy says): *Je suis Nigérian. *(zhuh swee Nee-jay-ree-ahn)

- I am Nigerian (a girl says): *Je suis Nigériane. *(zhuh swee Nee-jay-ree-ahn) Notice the extra 'e' at the end for a girl.

This rule is common: for many nationalities, we add an *'e' *for the feminine form.

**Basic Personal Pronouns (Les Pronoms Personnels): **Pronouns are small words we use instead of repeating a name. They are the subject of a sentence (who is doing the action).

- *Je *(zhuh) - *I*

- *Tu *(too) - *You *(informal, singular)

- *Il *(eel) - *He*

- *Elle *(ell) - *She*

**Important: ***Vous *(voo) can mean *"You" *(formal, to one person) OR *"You" *(plural, to a group).

# WEEK 7: SE PRÉSENTER (INTRODUCING ONESELF) - PART 2.

**OBJECTIVES **By the end of the lesson, pupils should be able to:

- Count from 1 to 20 in French.

- State their age in French.

- Ask someone "How old are you?".

- Identify their gender (Boy or Girl) in French.

**CONTENT Numbers 1-20 (Les Nombres de 1 à 20): **Numbers are very useful! We need them for age, counting, and later for telling time. Let's learn them with a rhythm or a song to make it easier.

- **1-10: **Un (uh), Deux (duh), Trois (twah), Quatre (kat-ruh), Cinq (sank), Six (sees), Sept (set), Huit (weet), Neuf (nuhf), Dix (dees).

- **11-15: **Onze (onz), Douze (dooz), Treize (trez), Quatorze (kah-torz), Quinze (kanz).

- **16-20: **Seize (sez), Dix-sept (dee-set), Dix-huit (dee-zweet), Dix-neuf (dee-nuhf), Vingt (van).

**Pronunciation Tip: **For 17, 18, 19, think "Ten-seven", "Ten-eight", "Ten-nine". The 'x' in *dix*

becomes a 'z' sound before *sept *and *huit*.

**Stating Your Age (Quel âge as-tu?): **In English, we say "I am 9 years old." In French, we use the verb *"avoir" *(to have). We say "I have 9 years."

- **Question (Informal): ***Quel âge as-tu? *(kel ahzh ah too?) - How old are you?

- **Answer: ***J'ai<u> </u>ans. *(zhay<u> </u>ahn) - I am<u> </u>years old.

- Example: *J'ai neuf ans. *(I am 9 years old.)

- Example: *J'ai dix ans. *(I am 10 years old.)

- *J'ai *is the contraction of *Je + ai *(I have).

**Stating Your Gender: **This is a simple but important part of introducing yourself.

- I am a boy: *Je suis un garçon. *(zhuh swee uhn gar-sohn)

- I am a girl: *Je suis une fille. *(zhuh swee oon fee-yuh)

**Note: **"Un" is used for masculine words (garçon), "une" for feminine words (fille).

# WEEK 8: PRENDRE CONGÉ (TAKING LEAVE) - PART 1.

**OBJECTIVES **By the end of the lesson, pupils should be able to:

- List three different ways to say goodbye based on time.

- Use expressions to wish someone well.

- Match the correct farewell to the situation.

**CONTENT More Ways to Say Goodbye: **We already know *"Au revoir"*. But French has special phrases for different situations, just like we might say "Safe journey!" in English.

- *Adieu *(ah-dyuh): This is a very formal and final *"Farewell"*. It implies you might not see the person for a very long time or ever again. Use it rarely.

- *Salut *(sah-loo): Remember, this friendly word means both *"Hi" *and *"Bye" *when talking to friends.

**Time-Specific Goodbyes: **These are useful because they tell the person when you expect to see them next.

- *À tout à l'heure! *(ah toot ah luhr) - *See you later! *(Used when you will see the person again *later the same day*, like after break time).

- *À ce soir! *(ah suh swahr) - *See you tonight! *(If you have plans to meet in the evening).

- *À la prochaine! *(ah lah proh-shen) - *Until next time! *(A friendly, general phrase for when you'll meet again, but you're not sure exactly when).

**Wishing Someone Well (Les Souhaits): **It is very polite and kind to wish someone a good day, weekend, or holiday as you leave.

- *Bonne journée! *(bun zhoor-nay) - *Have a good day! *(Say this during the daytime when you are leaving someone).

# WEEK 9: PRENDRE CONGÉ (TAKING LEAVE) - PART 2.

**OBJECTIVES **By the end of the lesson, pupils should be able to:

- Express gratitude when leaving a place.

- Construct a simple dialogue combining greetings and farewells.

- Demonstrate polite behavior when taking leave.

**CONTENT Expressing Gratitude (Remercier): **Before you say goodbye, it is very polite to thank the person for their time, help, or hospitality.

- *Merci beaucoup. *(mair-see boh-koo) - *Thank you very much.*

- *Merci pour tout. *(mair-see poor too) - *Thank you for everything. *(Great when leaving a party or a visit).

- *Merci bien. *(mair-see byehn) - *Thanks a lot. *(Another way to say thank you).

**Building a Complete, Polite Conversation: **A good conversation has a clear start, middle, and end. Let's build one step-by-step.

- **Greeting: **Start with a polite "Bonjour" or friendly "Salut".

- **Ask about well-being: **Show you care by asking "Comment ça va?"

- **Respond & Exchange: **Answer and ask back "Et toi?" or "Et vous?".

- **State your reason for leaving: **You can say "Je dois partir" (I must go) or just move to the goodbye.

- **Express thanks: **Say "Merci" or "Merci beaucoup".

- **Say goodbye: **Use an appropriate phrase like "Au revoir" or "À demain".

- **Wish them well: **End with "Bonne journée!" etc.

**Example Dialogue**

- **Tunde: **Bonjour, Maman. (Hello, Mum.)

- **Maman: **Bonjour, Tunde. Ça va? (Hello, Tunde. How are you?)

- **Tunde: **Ça va bien, merci. Je vais jouer chez Ibrahim. (I'm fine, thank you. I'm going to play at Ibrahim's house.)

- **Maman: **D'accord. Sois prudent! (Okay. Be careful!)

- **Tunde: **Merci, Maman. Au revoir! (Thank you, Mum. Goodbye!)

- **Maman: **Au revoir, mon fils. À tout à l'heure! (Goodbye, my son. See you later!)

**Polite Behavior: **When taking leave from an adult, remember to speak clearly, make eye contact, and smile. It shows respect.

**WEEK 1: PRÉSENTER LES MEMBRES DE LA FAMILLE PROCHE (PRESENTING CLOSE FAMILY MEMBERS) - PART 1.**

**OBJECTIVES **By the end of the lesson, pupils should be able to:

- List the French names for close family members (Father, Mother, Brother, Sister).

- Use the correct possessive adjectives ("mon" and "ma") to introduce family members.

- Introduce a family member using "Voici" or "C'est".

- Construct simple sentences stating family members they have.

**CONTENT Introduction to Family Vocabulary (La Famille): **The family is the most important group of people in our lives. In French, we call it *"la famille" *(lah fah-mee). Today, we learn the names of the people who live in our home.

- *Le père *(luh pair) / *Papa *- The father / Dad. Think of your dad, the head of the family.

- *La mère *(lah mair) / *Maman *- The mother / Mom. Think of your mum, who cares for everyone.

- *Le frère *(luh frair) - The brother. This is any boy who shares the same parents as you.

- *La sœur *(lah sur) - The sister. This is any girl who shares the same parents as you.

**Saying "My" in French: Possessive Adjectives (Mon / Ma): **In English, we say "my father" and "my mother." The word "my" doesn't change. In French, the word for "my" *changes *depending on whether the family member is male or female.

- *Mon *(mohn): This means *"my" *and is used *before masculine words*. *Père *and *frère *are masculine.

- *Mon père *(my father)

- *Mon frère *(my brother)

- *Ma *(mah): This means *"my" *and is used *before feminine words*. *Mère *and *sœur *are feminine.

- *Ma mère *(my mother)

- *Ma sœur *(my sister)

**Memory Trick: ***Mon *sounds a bit like "man" (for males). *Ma *sounds like "mama" (for females).

**Introducing Your Family (Voici / C'est): **When you want to show someone a picture or point out a family member, you use special phrases:

- *Voici... *(vwah-see): This means *"Here is..."*. It's perfect when you are showing a photo or presenting someone.

- Example: *Voici mon père. *(Here is my father.)

- *C'est... *(say): This means *"This is..." *or *"He/She is..."*. It's a very common and natural way to introduce someone.

- Example: *C'est ma mère. *(This is my mother.)

**Talking About What You Have (J'ai...): **To say who is in your family, you use the verb

*"avoir" *(to have). The form for "I have" is *"J'ai" *(zhay).

- *J'ai un frère. *(I have a brother.) - *'Un' *is used for one masculine thing/person.

- *J'ai une sœur. *(I have a sister.) - *'Une' *is used for one feminine thing/person.

- *J'ai deux sœurs. *(I have two sisters.) - For more than one, we don't use *un/une*.

# WEEK 3: PRÉSENTER LES MEMBRES DE LA FAMILLE PROCHE (PRESENTING CLOSE FAMILY MEMBERS) - PART 3.

**OBJECTIVES **By the end of the lesson, pupils should be able to:

- Use the verb "avoir" (to have) correctly in the singular forms (J'ai, Tu as, Il a, Elle a).

- Describe family members using basic adjectives (grand, petit, jeune, vieux).

- Explain simple family relationships.

- Construct a full sentence describing their own family composition.

**CONTENT The Verb "Avoir" (To Have) - More Forms: **We know *"J'ai" *(I have). Now let's learn how to say "you have," "he has," and "she has." This is called conjugating the verb.

- *J'ai *(zhay) - I have.

- *Tu as *(too ah) - You have (informal, to a friend).

- *Il a *(eel ah) - He has.

- *Elle a *(el ah) - She has.

Examples:

- *Tu as un frère? *(Do you have a brother?)

- *Il a une sœur. *(He has a sister.)

- *Elle a deux cousins. *(She has two cousins.)

**Describing Your Family (Les Adjectifs): **We can use simple words to describe what our family members are like.

- *Grand(e) *(grahn/grahnd) - *Big/Tall*. (Add 'e' for feminine: *ma sœur est grande*).

- *Petit(e) *(puh-tee/ puh-teet) - *Small/Short*. (Add 'e' for feminine).

- *Jeune *(zhuhn) - *Young*.

- *Vieux / Vieille *(vyuh / vyeh-ee) - *Old*. (Vieux for masculine, Vieille for feminine).

**How to use them: **Place the adjective *after *the verb *"est" *(is).

- *Mon père est grand. *(My father is tall.)

- *Ma sœur est petite. *(My sister is small/short.)

- *Mon grand-père est vieux. *(My grandfather is old.)

**Understanding Family Relationships: **Let's learn how to explain who is who in the family.

- *Mes parents *(may pah-rahn) - My parents (father and mother together).

- *Le père de mon père est mon grand-père. *(The father of my father is my grandfather.) This is a logical way to think about relationships.

**WEEK 1: COMPTER DE 0 À 20 (COUNTING FROM 0 TO 20) - PART 1.**

**OBJECTIVES **By the end of the lesson, pupils should be able to:

- Count from 0 to 10 in French with correct pronunciation.

- Count at least five objects in the classroom using French numbers.

- Perform simple addition sums in French (e.g., 2 + 3 = 5).

- Write the French spelling for numbers 0-10.

**CONTENT Numbers 0-10 (Les Nombres de Zéro à Dix): **Numbers are the foundation for so many things—telling time, giving your age, and even buying things at the market. Let's start with the basics.

- **0: Zéro **(zay-roh) - Like the "zero" in a football score.

- **1: Un **(uh) - Sounds like the beginning of "under".

- **2: Deux **(duh) - Rhymes with "duh". Lips are rounded.

- **3: Trois **(twah) - Sounds like "twah". The 'r' is soft.

- **4: Quatre **(kat-ruh) - Sounds like "cat" with an 'r'. The 'e' is silent.

- **5: Cinq **(sank) - Sounds like "sank" a ship. The 'q' is silent.

- **6: Six **(sees) - Sounds like the English word "seas".

- **7: Sept **(set) - Sounds like the English word "set".

- **8: Huit **(weet) - Sounds like "weet". The 'h' is silent.

- **9: Neuf **(nuhf) - Sounds like "nuh" with a soft 'f' at the end.

- **10: Dix **(dees) - Sounds like "dees". The 'x' sounds like 's'.

**Pronunciation is Key: **Let's practice as a class, using claps or stamps for each number to feel the rhythm.

**Counting Real Objects (Compter les Objets): **Now, let's use our numbers to count things we can see. Point to objects as you count.

- *Un stylo. *(One pen.)

- *Deux cahiers. *(Two notebooks.)

- *Trois livres. *(Three books.)

- *Quatre élèves. *(Four pupils.)

- *Cinq chaises. *(Five chairs.)

**Activity: **The teacher holds up a number of pencils. Pupils shout out the correct French number.

**Simple Math in French (Les Maths Simples): **In French, we use the word *"plus" *(ploo) for addition and *"égale" *(ay-gal) for equals.

- *Un plus un égale deux. *(1 + 1 = 2)

- *Deux plus trois égale cinq. *(2 + 3 = 5)

- *Cinq plus quatre égale neuf. *(5 + 4 = 9)

**Think of it like a recipe: ***Two *mangoes *plus three more mangoes equals *a bowl of *five *mangoes!

4.  Write the French word for the number *10*. (Dix).

5.  How do you say "Zero" in French, like when a team has no goals? (Zéro).

# WEEK 2: COMPTER DE 0 À 20 (COUNTING FROM 0 TO 20) - PART 2.

**OBJECTIVES **By the end of the lesson, pupils should be able to:

- Count from 11 to 20 in French.

- Ask "How many?" using the phrase "Combien de...?".

- State their age using French numbers (J'ai... ans).

- Count objects up to 20.

**CONTENT Numbers 11-20 (Les Nombres de Onze à Vingt): **Let's continue our number journey! Notice a pattern from 17 to 19: they are like "ten-seven," "ten-eight," "ten-nine."

- **11: Onze **(onz) - Sounds like "onz".

- **12: Douze **(dooz) - Rhymes with "ooze".

- **13: Treize **(trez) - Sounds like "trez". The 'ei' sounds like 'e' in "bed".

- **14: Quatorze **(kah-torz) - Sounds like "kah-torz".

- **15: Quinze **(kanz) - Sounds like "kanz".

- **16: Seize **(sez) - Sounds like "says" without the 'y'.

- **17: Dix-sept **(dee-set) - Literally "ten-seven."

- **18: Dix-huit **(dee-zweet) - "Ten-eight." The 'x' in *dix *becomes a 'z' sound here.

- **19: Dix-neuf **(dee-nuhf) - "Ten-nine."

- **20: Vingt **(van) - Sounds like "van" with a soft 't'. The 'g' is silent.

**Asking "How Many?" (Combien?): **This is a very useful question for shopping, sharing, and playing games.

- *Combien? *(kohm-byehn) - *How many? / How much?*

- *Combien de...? *(kohm-byehn duh) - *How many \[things\]?*

- Example: *Combien de livres? *(How many books?)

- Answer: *Cinq livres. *(Five books.)

- Example: *Combien de frères as-tu? *(How many brothers do you have?)

**Using Numbers in Real Sentences: **Let's use these numbers in sentences we already know.

- **Age: ***J'ai douze ans. *(I am twelve years old.) - Remember, we use *"J'ai" *(I have) for age.

- **Family: ***J'ai deux sœurs. *(I have two sisters.)

- **Classroom: ***Il y a vingt élèves dans ma classe. *(There are twenty pupils in my class.) *"Il y a" *(eel ee ah) means "there is/are".

# WEEK 3: DIRE L'HEURE (TELLING TIME) - PART 1.

**OBJECTIVES **By the end of the lesson, pupils should be able to:

- Ask "What time is it?" in French.

- Tell the time on the hour (1 o'clock, 2 o'clock, etc.).

- Identify the parts of the day (morning, afternoon, evening, night).

- State the time using "du matin" or "du soir".

**CONTENT Asking for the Time (Demander l'Heure): **Time helps us know when to go to school, eat, or play. The word for time is *"l'heure" *(lur).

- **Question: ***Quelle heure est-il? *(kel lur ay-teel) - *What time is it? *You can think of it as "Which hour is it?"

**Telling Time on the Hour (L'Heure Pile): **We tell time using *"Il est" *(eel ay) which means "It is." For times on the hour, we say the number plus *"heure(s)"*.

- **1:00: ***Il est une heure. *(It is one o'clock.) - *Une *is used because *heure *is feminine.

- **2:00: ***Il est deux heures. *(It is two o'clock.) - For 2 and above, add an 's' to *heure*.

- **3:00: ***Il est trois heures.*

- **10:00: ***Il est dix heures.*

- **12:00 (Noon): ***Il est midi. *(mee-dee)

- **12:00 (Midnight): ***Il est minuit. *(mee-nwee)

**Special Note: **For 1 o'clock, we use *"une heure"*. For all others (2, 3, 4...), we use *"deux heures, trois heures..."*.

**Parts of the Day (Les Moments de la Journée): **We add these phrases to make the time more clear, especially since 6:00 could be morning or evening!

- *Le matin *(luh mah-tan) - *The morning *(from sunrise to about 12 PM).

- *L'après-midi *(lah-preh-mee-dee) - *The afternoon *(from 12 PM to about 6 PM).

- *Le soir *(luh swahr) - *The evening *(from about 6 PM to bedtime).

- *La nuit *(lah nwee) - *The night *(when it is dark and people sleep).

**Putting It All Together**

- *Il est huit heures du matin. *(It is 8 o'clock in the morning.) - Time for school!

- *Il est quatre heures de l'après-midi. *(It is 4 o'clock in the afternoon.) - Time for homework or play.

- *Il est sept heures du soir. *(It is 7 o'clock in the evening.) - Time for dinner or family time.

# WEEK 4: DIRE L'HEURE (TELLING TIME) - PART 2.

**OBJECTIVES **By the end of the lesson, pupils should be able to:

- Tell time with half hours ("et demie").

- Tell time with quarter past ("et quart").

- Tell time with quarter to ("moins le quart").

- Describe a simple daily routine using time.

**CONTENT Half Past, Quarter Past, and Quarter To: **Now let's learn to tell time more precisely, just like we look at the minute hand on a clock.

- **Half Past (30 minutes): ***et demie *(ay duh-mee).

- Example: *Il est deux heures et demie. *(It is 2:30.)

- **Quarter Past (15 minutes): ***et quart *(ay kar).

- Example: *Il est trois heures et quart. *(It is 3:15.)

- **Quarter To (45 minutes): ***moins le quart *(mwan luh kar). This means "minus the quarter." We say the *next *hour, minus a quarter.

- Example: *Il est quatre heures moins le quart. *(It is 3:45.) Literally: "It is four o'clock minus a quarter."

**Think of it like a cake: **If you have a cake cut into 4 quarters, *et quart *means you have one piece. *Moins le quart *means one piece is missing from the full cake (the next hour).

**Daily Routines with Time (La Routine Quotidienne): **Let's talk about what we do at different times of the day. This is called our routine.

- *Je me réveille à six heures. *(zhuh muh ray-vay ah see zur) - *I wake up at 6 o'clock.*

- *Je vais à l'école à sept heures et demie. *- *I go to school at 7:30.*

- *Je mange à midi. *(zhuh mahnzh ah mee-dee) - *I eat at noon. *(Lunch)

- *Je fais mes devoirs à quatre heures. *(zhuh fay may duh-vwahr) - *I do my homework at 4 o'clock.*

- *Je dîne à huit heures. *(zhuh deen ah weet ur) - *I have dinner at 8 o'clock.*

# WEEK 7: IDENTIFIER LES ALIMENTS ET LES REPAS (IDENTIFYING FOODS AND MEALS) - PART 1.

**OBJECTIVES **By the end of the lesson, pupils should be able to:

- Name the three main meals of the day in French.

- List at least five common food items (bread, rice, meat, etc.).

- Identify common fruits and vegetables in French.

- Construct simple sentences using "Je mange..." (I eat...).

**CONTENT Meals of the Day (Les Repas): **We eat at different times to give our bodies energy. The names of meals in French are different from English.

- *Le petit-déjeuner *(luh puh-tee day-zhuh-nay) - *Breakfast. *Literally "the little lunch." This is the first meal.

- *Le déjeuner *(luh day-zhuh-nay) - *Lunch. *The main meal in the middle of the day.

- *Le dîner *(luh dee-nay) - *Dinner. *The evening meal.

- *Manger *(mahn-zhay) - *To eat.*

- *Boire *(bwahr) - *To drink.*

**Common Foods (Les Aliments Courants): **These are foods you probably know and eat often.

- *Le pain *(luh pan) - *Bread.*

- *Le riz *(luh ree) - *Rice. *(A staple in Nigeria!)

- *La viande *(lah vee-ahnd) - *Meat.*

- *Le poisson *(luh pwah-sohn) - *Fish.*

- *L'œuf *(luhf) - *Egg. *(One egg). *Les œufs *(lay zuh) - *Eggs.*

**Fruits and Vegetables (Les Fruits et Les Légumes): **Eating fruits and vegetables keeps us healthy. Many names are similar to English!

- **Fruits: ***La banane *(banana), *L'orange *(orange), *La pomme *(pom - apple), *La mangue*

(mahng - mango).

- **Vegetables: ***La tomate *(tomato), *La carotte *(kah-rot - carrot), *Les épinards *(lay zay-pee- nar - spinach), *L'oignon *(loh-nyon - onion).

**Saying What You Eat (Je mange...): **To say you eat something, use *"Je mange" *(zhuh mahnzh)

\+ the food.

- *Je mange du pain. *(I eat bread.) *'Du' *is used for masculine foods (like pain, riz).

- *Je mange de la viande. *(I eat meat.) *'De la' *is used for feminine foods (like viande).

- *Je mange une pomme. *(I eat an apple.) *'Une' *for one feminine item.

- *Je mange un œuf. *(I eat an egg.) *'Un' *for one masculine item.

- *L'eau *(loh) - *Water. *(Feminine: *de l'eau*).

- *Le lait *(luh lay) - *Milk.*

- *Le jus *(luh zhoo) - *Juice. Le jus d'orange *(orange juice).

- *Le thé *(luh tay) - *Tea.*

- To drink *is "boire" *(bwahr). *Je bois de l'eau. *(I drink water.)

# WEEK 9: IDENTIFIER LES ALIMENTS ET LES REPAS (IDENTIFYING FOODS AND MEALS) - PART 3.

**OBJECTIVES **By the end of the lesson, pupils should be able to:

- Use polite expressions to order food ("Je voudrais...").

- Ask for the price of an item ("Combien ça coûte?").

- Use quantity words (a little, a lot).

# CONTENT

**Ordering Food Politely (Commander de la Nourriture): **Imagine you are in a French café or a market. How do you ask for what you want?

- **Polite Request: ***Je voudrais... *(zhuh voo-dray) - *I would like... *This is much more polite than "Je veux" (I want).

- Always add: *...s'il vous plaît. *(seel voo pleh) - *...please.*

- Example: *Je voudrais un jus d'orange, s'il vous plaît. *(I would like an orange juice, please.)

- Example: *Je voudrais du pain, s'il vous plaît.*

**Asking the Price (Demander le Prix): **Before you buy, you need to know how much it costs.

- *Combien ça coûte? *(kohm-byehn sah koot?) - *How much does it cost?*

- *C'est combien? *(say kohm-byehn?) - *How much is it? *(More informal)

- The seller might answer: "C'est cent nairas." (It is 100 naira.) or "Dix nairas." (10 naira.)

**Talking About Quantities: **Sometimes you want a lot, sometimes just a little.

- *Un peu de... *(uh puh duh) - *A little bit of...*

- *Je voudrais un peu de sucre. *(I would like a little sugar.)

- *Beaucoup de... *(boh-koo duh) - *A lot of...*

- *Je voudrais beaucoup de riz. *(I would like a lot of rice.)

- *Un kilo de... *(uh kee-loh duh) - *A kilo of...*

**Good Manners at the Table: **In French culture, it is very important to wish people a good meal.

- **Before eating: ***Bon appétit! *(bohn ah-pay-tee) - *Enjoy your meal! *Say this to everyone at the table.

- **After eating: ***C'était délicieux! *(say-tay day-lee-syuh) - *It was delicious! *A nice compliment to the cook.
