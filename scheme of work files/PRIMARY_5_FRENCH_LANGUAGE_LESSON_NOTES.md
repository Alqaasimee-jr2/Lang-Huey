**PRIMARY 5 FRENCH LANGUAGE LESSON NOTES FIRST TERM**

**WEEK 1: RÉVISION - LES SALUTATIONS ET L'ALPHABET**

**OBJECTIVES **By the end of the lesson, pupils should be able to:

1.  Recite the French alphabet correctly, knowing the key sounds that are different from English.

2.  Exchange appropriate greetings in French for different times of the day and situations.

3.  Count reliably from 1 to 20 in French.

4.  Introduce themselves simply using "Je m'appelle" and state their age using "J'ai ... ans".

# CONTENT

1.  **L'Alphabet (The Alphabet) **The French alphabet has the same 26 letters as the English alphabet, but many letters sound very different. It is like learning new sounds for old friends.

    - **The Key Difference: **In French, we say the letter names, not the sounds they make in words. For example, the letter 'A' is called "ah", not "ay".

    - **Pronunciation Guide: **Let's learn the names. Listen and repeat.

      - A (ah), B (bay), C (say), D (day), E (euh - like the 'u' in 'up'), F (eff), G (jay), H (ash)

      - I (ee), J (ji), K (kah), L (ell), M (em), N (en), O (oh), P (pay)

      - Q (ku), R (air - a soft sound from your throat), S (ess), T (tay), U (ew - purse your lips), V (vay), W (doobl-vay)

      - X (eeks), Y (ee-grek), Z (zed).

<!-- -->

1.  **Les Salutations (Greetings) **Greetings are our first words to someone. We use different ones depending on the time and who we are talking to.

    - **Bonjour: **Good morning / Hello. We use this from when we wake up until the evening. We say it to our teacher, parents, and strangers. (Example: You walk into class and say "Bonjour, Monsieur/Madame" to your teacher.)

    - **Bonsoir: **Good evening. We use this when it starts to get dark, from late afternoon.

    - **Salut: **Hi / Hello. This is informal, for friends and people you know very well. (Example: Meeting your friend at the playground.)

    - **Au revoir: **Goodbye. A polite way to leave anyone.

    - **Bonne nuit: **Good night. Only used when someone is going to sleep.

    - **Comment ça va?: **How are you? A friendly question.

    - **Ça va bien, merci: **I am fine, thank you. A good reply.

1.  **Se Présenter (Introducing Oneself) **When you meet someone new, you tell them who you are.

    - **My name is... : **Je m'appelle \[Name\]. This means "I call myself".

      - Example: Je m'appelle Chinedu. (My name is Chinedu).

# WEEK 2: COMPTER DE 21 – 60 (I)

**OBJECTIVES **By the end of the lesson, pupils should be able to:

1.  Count and spell numbers from 21 to 30 in French correctly.

2.  Understand the pattern of using "vingt-et-un" and "vingt-deux".

3.  Perform and state simple addition (like 20+5) in French.

4.  Ask "How many?" using the word "Combien?" in a simple question.

# CONTENT

1.  **Les Nombres 21 – 30 (Numbers 21-30) **After 20, numbers follow a clear pattern. Think of it as building with blocks: the tens block (20) plus a units block (1, 2, 3...).

    - **The Special One: **For 21, we join 20 and 1 with "et" (and): Vingt-et-un.

    - **The Pattern: **For 22 to 29, we join 20 and the unit with a hyphen (-): Vingt-deux, Vingt- trois, Vingt-quatre...

    - **The New Ten: **30 is a new word: Trente.

1.  **Asking "Combien?" (How many?) **When you want to know the quantity of something, you ask "Combien?".

    - **Structure: **Combien de + item?

    - **Example: **Combien de crayons? (How many pencils?)

    - **Answer: **You say the number, then the item.

`o`` `Example: Vingt-cinq crayons. (25 pencils). (Imagine counting pencils in a jar.)

1.  **Simple Calculations (Les Calculs) **We can do small maths in French! Here are the words we need:

 **+ (Plus) **is "plus".

 **= (Equals) **is "égale".

- **Example 1: **20 + 2 = 22 → Vingt plus deux égale vingt-deux. (We are adding 2 pencils to 20).

- **Example 2: **10 + 15 = 25 → Dix plus quinze égale vingt-cinq. (We are adding money:

₦10 + ₦15 = ₦25).

# WEEK 3: COMPTER DE 21 – 60 (II)

**OBJECTIVES **By the end of the lesson, pupils should be able to:

1.  Count and write numbers from 31 to 50 in French.

2.  Use these numbers to talk about prices in Naira and people's ages.

3.  State a simple Nigerian phone number digit-by-digit in French.

# CONTENT

    - **Special One: **31 is Trente-et-un (30 and 1).

    - **The Pattern: **32 to 39: Trente-deux, Trente-trois... Trente-neuf.

    - **New Ten: **40 is Quarante.

    - **Special One: **41 is Quarante-et-un.

    - **The Pattern: **42 to 49: Quarante-deux, Quarante-trois... Quarante-neuf.

    - **New Ten: **50 is Cinquante.

1.  **Practical Use of Numbers **Numbers are not just for counting; we use them every day!

    - **Age: **To say someone's age, we use "a ... ans".

      - Example: Mon père a quarante-cinq ans. (My father is 45 years old).

    <!-- -->

    - **Money/Prices: **In Nigeria, we use Naira (N). In French, we say the number, then "Nairas".

      - Example: \#40 = Quarante Nairas. \#50 = Cinquante Nairas. (Like the price of a snack.)

    - **Phone Numbers: **We say each digit separately. "Zéro" is 0.

      - Example: 0803 12 34 567 → Zéro, huit, zéro, trois, un, deux, trois, quatre, cinq, six, sept.

# WEEK 4: COMPTER DE 21 – 60 (III)

**OBJECTIVES **By the end of the lesson, pupils should be able to:

    - **New Ten: **60 is Soixante. (Pronounced "swa-sont").

1.  **Simple Subtraction (La Soustraction) **Now let's learn to "take away" in French.

    - **- (Minus) **is "moins".

    - **Example 1: **60 - 10 = 50 → Soixante moins dix égale cinquante. (If you have 60 biscuits and eat 10, you have 50 left).

    - **Example 2: **55 - 5 = 50 → Cinquante-cinq moins cinq égale cinquante. (You have 55 kobo and spend 5 kobo).

**WEEK 7: PRÉSENTER LES AUTRES (I)**

**OBJECTIVES **By the end of the lesson, pupils should be able to:

1.  Use "Voici" and "C'est" correctly to introduce another person.

2.  Clearly differentiate between "Il" (He) for males and "Elle" (She) for females.

3.  Introduce a friend or family member by stating their name using "Il/Elle s'appelle".

4.  State a simple relationship like "mon ami" or "ma sœur".

# CONTENT

1.  **Introductory Phrases: "Voici" vs. "C'est" **When you want to present someone to others, you have two main phrases:

    - **Voici... **means "Here is..." or "This is...". It's like pointing someone out.

      - Example: Voici mon ami, Tunde. (Here is my friend, Tunde). (Imagine Tunde is standing next to you.)

    <!-- -->

    - **C'est... **also means "This is..." or "It is...". It's used more for identification.

      - Example: C'est Amina. C'est ma sœur. (This is Amina. She is my sister).

<!-- -->

1.  **Subject Pronouns: Il vs. Elle **In English, we use "he" and "she". In French:

    - **Il = He. **Used for boys, men, and male animals.

    - **Elle = She. **Used for girls, women, and female animals.

1.  **Stating Someone Else's Name **To say "His name is" or "Her name is", we use "s'appelle".

    - **His name is... : **Il s'appelle \[Name\].

      - Example: Il s'appelle Chukwuemeka.

    <!-- -->

    - **Her name is... : **Elle s'appelle \[Name\].

      - Example: Elle s'appelle Ngozi.

1.  **Simple Relationships **You can say how you know the person.

    - Mon ami (My male friend). Mon amie (My female friend - hear the extra 'e'?).

    - Mon frère (My brother). Ma sœur (My sister).

    - **Full Example: **Voici Bola. C'est mon amie. Elle s'appelle Bola.

# WEEK 8: PRÉSENTER LES AUTRES (II)

**OBJECTIVES **By the end of the lesson, pupils should be able to:

1.  State the age of another person using "Il a ... ans" or "Elle a ... ans".

# CONTENT

1.  **Stating Someone Else's Age**

Remember "J'ai ... ans" for yourself? For others, we use the same verb "avoir" (to have) but with Il/Elle.

- **He is \[X\] years old: **Il a \[X\] ans.

  - Example: Il a dix ans. (He is 10).

<!-- -->

- **She is \[X\] years old: **Elle a \[X\] ans.

  - Example: Elle a onze ans. (She is 11).

1.  **Stating Nationality **To say where someone is from, we use "Il est" or "Elle est".

    - **Masculine (for males): **Il est nigérian. (He is Nigerian). Il est français. (He is French).

    - **Feminine (for females): **We usually add an 'e'.

      - Elle est nigériane. (She is Nigerian).

      - Elle est française. (She is French).

<!-- -->

    - **Tall: **Il est grand. / Elle est grande. (Think of a tall basketball player.)

    - **Short: **Il est petit. / Elle est petite. (Think of a younger sibling.)

    - **Kind/Nice: **Il est gentil. / Elle est gentille. (Like a helpful friend.)

    - You: Il est nigérian. (He is Nigerian).

# WEEK 10: IDENTIFIER LES PARTIES DU CORPS HUMAIN (II)

**OBJECTIVES **By the end of the lesson, pupils should be able to:

1.  Identify and name major parts of the body (from neck to feet) in French.

2.  Distinguish between similar parts: 'le bras' (arm) and 'la main' (hand); 'la jambe' (leg) and 'le pied' (foot).

3.  Express simple pains using the very useful phrase "J'ai mal à..." (I have pain at...).

4.  Correctly label a body diagram.

# CONTENT

1.  **Le Corps (The Body) **Let's move down from the head. Here are the main body parts.

    - **Le cou: **The neck (connects head to body).

    - **Le bras: **The arm (from shoulder to hand).

    - **La main: **The hand (at the end of the arm).

    - **Le doigt: **The finger (on the hand).

    - **Le ventre: **The stomach/belly. (Where we feel hungry!)

    - **Le dos: **The back.

    - **La jambe: **The leg (from hip to foot).

    - **Le genou: **The knee (in the middle of the leg).

# CONTENT

1.  **Le Vocabulaire de la Routine (Routine Vocabulary) **Your routine quotidienne (daily routine) is all the things you do every day from morning to night. In French, many actions you do to yourself (like washing) use special verbs that start with "se".

    - **Se réveiller: **To wake up. (When your eyes open in the morning).

    - **Se lever: **To get up, to rise from the bed.

    - **Se laver: **To wash yourself. (This can mean washing your face or having a bath).

    - **Se brosser les dents: **To brush your teeth.

    - **S'habiller: **To get dressed. (Putting on your school uniform).

    - **Prendre le petit déjeuner: **To have/take breakfast. (Eating your morning meal).

1.  **Forming Sentences (Je me...) **When you talk about yourself doing these actions, the "se" changes to "me". It's like the action is bouncing back to you!

    - **Je me réveille. **(I wake up). ("Je" + "me" + "réveille")

    - **Je me lave. **(I wash myself).

    - **Je m'habille. **(I get dressed). We say "m'habille" because 'habille' starts with a vowel (h is silent).

    - **Le matin: **The morning. (From waking up until about 12 noon).

    - **L'après-midi: **The afternoon. (From after 12 noon until evening).

    - **Le soir: **The evening. (From late afternoon until bedtime).

    - **Example Sentence: **Le matin, je me lave. (In the morning, I wash myself).

# WEEK 10: LES ALIMENTS ET LES REPAS

**OBJECTIVES **By the end of the lesson, pupils should be able to:

1.  List five common food items in French.

2.  Name the three main meals of the day in French.

3.  Express hunger ("J'ai faim") and thirst ("J'ai soif") correctly.

4.  State one food or drink they like using "J'aime..." or "Je veux manger..."

# CONTENT

1.  **Les Aliments (Food Items) **Les aliments are what we eat and drink to grow strong and healthy.

    - **Le pain: **Bread. (Like agege bread!).

    - **Le riz: **Rice. (A staple food in Nigeria!).

    - **La viande: **Meat.

    - **Le poisson: **Fish.

    - **Les fruits: **Fruits. (Like mango - la mangue, orange - l'orange).

    - **Les légumes: **Vegetables. (Like spinach - les épinards).

    - **L'eau: **Water. (Essential for life!).

    - **Le jus: **Juice. (Orange juice = le jus d'orange).

<!-- -->

1.  **Les Repas (Meals) **We eat these foods at specific times, called meals.

    - **Le petit déjeuner: **Breakfast. (The first, small meal to 'break' the 'fast').

    - **Le déjeuner: **Lunch. (The midday meal).

    - **Le dîner: **Dinner. (The evening meal).

1.  **Expressing Hunger and Thirst **When your body needs food or water, you feel a sensation. In French, we say we "have" hunger or thirst.

    - **J'ai faim: **I am hungry. (Literally: I have hunger).

    - **J'ai soif: **I am thirsty. (Literally: I have thirst).
