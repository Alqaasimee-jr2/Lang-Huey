# LinguaBoard — Strategic Insight
## Why the Landing Page. What It's For. Who It's For.
**Document Type:** Internal Strategic Brief  
**Author:** Abdullah Qaasim  
**Date:** August 2025

---

## The Honest Truth About Where We Are

LinguaBoard does not exist yet as a product. There is no app. There is no content. There is no French audio recorded. There is no license system built.

What exists is:
- A clear, validated problem
- A founder with the technical ability to build the solution
- A brand system and product vision
- A prelaunch landing page

This is not a weakness. This is the correct order of operations. The landing page is not a placeholder while the product gets built. **The landing page IS the first deliverable.** Its job is to find out if the product should be built at all — and if so, to ensure that the schools who need it most are already waiting when it launches.

This document explains the strategic logic behind that decision.

---

## Part 1: Why We Build the Landing Page Before the App

### 1.1 The Cost of Building Wrong

Building a Flutter app, recording 24 lessons of French audio, encrypting content packs, building a license key system, designing a smartboard UI — conservatively, this is 3 months of focused work at 2–3 hours per day. 

If we build first and then discover that:
- Schools in Lagos don't have Android smartboards
- The ₦75,000 price point is too high for the market
- Decision makers are not proprietors but school boards
- Schools prefer subscription over one-time payment

...then 3 months of work is wasted. Not partially useful. Wasted.

The landing page costs 2 weeks to design and build. It answers all of these questions before a single line of app code is written.

### 1.2 What the Landing Page Validates

Every registration on the prelaunch form is a data point:
- **Who registers** — are these proprietors? Principals? IT staff? Teachers? Tells us if our targeting is correct.
- **How many register** — volume tells us whether there is genuine interest or polite curiosity
- **What they say** — the suggestion field (if we add one) tells us what the market actually wants vs what we assumed
- **Where they come from** — if most registrations come from WhatsApp shares rather than Google, that tells us our go-to-market channel

A landing page with zero registrations after 4 weeks of active promotion is a signal to re-examine the product positioning before investing in a build. A landing page with 50+ registrations in 2 weeks is a green light with an audience already waiting.

### 1.3 Deposits Are the Real Validator

The landing page generates registrations. Registrations generate conversations. Conversations generate deposit commitments. **Two schools paying a ₦20,000–₦37,500 deposit is the actual build trigger — not registration count.**

A school that fills in a form is interested. A school that pays a deposit is a customer. We build for customers, not interest.

## Part 2: What the Landing Page Is Actually Doing

It is performing four jobs simultaneously, in order of priority.

### Job 1: Qualifying the Buyer
Not every school owner who visits is the right buyer. The right buyer:
- Has a smartboard already
- Currently cannot staff French classes
- Has budget authority (proprietor or principal, not staff)
- Is open to software solutions

The page copy is deliberately specific — "Android smartboards", "JSS1 French", "NERDC curriculum" — because this specificity repels the wrong visitor and attracts the right one. A school with no smartboard reads "Android smartboard" and self-selects out. That is correct behaviour. We want qualified registrations, not maximum registrations.

### Job 2: Building Trust Before the Product Exists
When a school owner lands on this page, they have never heard of LinguaBoard. They have no reason to trust it. The page must do trust work without the usual trust signals — no testimonials (no customers), no case studies (no pilots), no ratings (no app yet).

The trust signals we use instead:
- **The founder story** — a named individual, a real mother's school, a specific Lagos problem. This is verifiable and human.
- **Curriculum specificity** — saying "NERDC scheme of work, JSS1 French, 3 terms" tells a school owner that the builder understands their world. A foreign generic edtech tool would never know to say this.
- **The prelaunch honesty** — we explicitly say "we are in active development." This is counterintuitive but effective. Schools that have been burned by bad software respect a founder who doesn't oversell.
- **One-time payment** — this removes a major trust barrier. Schools that have been trapped in subscription software they couldn't cancel respond to "pay once, own it forever."

### Job 3: Creating Community Before Customers
The framing "join the movement" and "building team" is deliberate. It is not marketing language — it is a genuine invitation.

A school that registers on this page and receives a message asking for their feedback on what the French curriculum should cover becomes emotionally invested in the product before it launches. When LinguaBoard goes live, they are not evaluating a new product — they are seeing their suggestions implemented. That school buys. That school refers.

The community layer transforms a mailing list into a founding cohort. Founding cohorts generate word of mouth that paid advertising cannot buy.

### Job 4: Compressing the Sales Cycle
Selling software to a Nigerian school proprietor cold — walking in the door for the first time — has a long sales cycle. The proprietor needs to see the product, consult with the principal, think about it, check the budget, get back to you, schedule a demo, and then decide.

A school that has already visited the landing page, read the founder story, filled in the form, and received two email updates from Abdullah about the product's progress — that school is warm. The in-person visit becomes a confirmation, not a pitch. The sales cycle compresses from 6 weeks to 2 weeks.

The landing page does the cold-to-warm work before the sales visit happens.

## Part 3: Who This Page Is For

### The Primary Person

**Name:** Mrs. Adaobi (representative persona)  
**Role:** Proprietor, mid-size private secondary school in Lekki, Lagos  
**School size:** 200–400 students, JSS1–SS3  
**Technology:** Purchased two Android smartboards 18 months ago for the "modern school" branding  
**Problem:** The French teacher who was there when the boards were installed left 8 months ago. Since then, the JSS1–SS3 French timetable slots are nominally covered by a maths teacher who shows YouTube videos when she remembers.  
**Pain:** She knows WAEC results in French are suffering. She can't find a replacement. The last two candidates from teaching agencies were unqualified. Virtual classes cost ₦80,000/month and dropped out twice due to NEPA.  
**Budget authority:** Full. She decides.  
**How she finds this page:** A fellow proprietor sends her a WhatsApp message with the link after seeing it shared in a school owners' group.  
**What she does on the page:** Reads the headline. Feels seen. Reads the founder note. Trusts it. Fills in the form.

This is not a hypothetical persona. This is the founder's mother, slightly fictionalised. She is real. Thousands of her exist in Lagos alone.

### The Secondary Person

**Name:** Mr. Emeka (representative persona)  
**Role:** Principal, international curriculum private school in Ikeja  
**School size:** 150–250 students, operates British curriculum  
**Technology:** Promethean interactive whiteboard (Windows-based — important caveat)  
**Problem:** French is on the timetable as an elective. The French teacher is part-time and has given notice. Parents pay ₦800,000/term and expect French delivery.  
**Pain:** He cannot tell parents that French is suspended. He needs a credible solution fast.  
**Budget authority:** Partial — needs proprietor sign-off above ₦100,000  
**How he finds this page:** Google search "French lesson software Nigeria"  
**What he does on the page:** Reads carefully. Notes "Android smartboard" — checks if they have Android. They don't (Promethean is Windows). He still fills in the form and asks in the WhatsApp message if a Windows version is planned.

This person is valuable even if they can't buy v1. His query is a product signal: Windows smartboard coverage is a real market need and should be on the product roadmap.

### Who This Page Is NOT For

- **Students** — they are not the buyer, they don't have budget, and the page is not designed for them. If a student finds this page, nothing on it speaks to them. That is correct.
- **Teachers** — specifically not the French teacher (who would feel threatened) and not non-specialist teachers (who are the user of the app but not the buyer of it). Teachers do not appear anywhere in the page copy as an audience.
- **Government officials** — the government school market is a real opportunity but it has a completely different procurement process, different timeline, and different trust signals. A separate government-facing sales approach is needed later. This page would confuse that buyer.
- **Foreign edtech investors** — this page is not an investor pitch. If an investor finds it, they can draw their own conclusions. But the copy is written for school owners, not for people with capital to deploy.

## Part 4: Why This Approach Is Right for Nigeria Specifically

### 4.1 WhatsApp is the Distribution Channel
In the Nigerian private school market, proprietors communicate primarily through WhatsApp groups — school owner associations, regional education groups, faith-based school networks. A landing page that generates strong word of mouth in these groups can reach hundreds of schools without a single naira spent on advertising.

The page is designed for this. The founder story is shareable. The "you are early" framing creates social currency — being first matters in this community.

### 4.2 Trust Is Personal Before It Is Institutional
Nigerian school proprietors do not trust brands. They trust people. The founder's name (Abdullah), the specific detail (mother's school in Lagos), and the direct communication style ("I built this for her — and now I'm building it for yours") is not accidental. It is the trust architecture for this market.

A Western edtech company entering Nigeria with a polished corporate landing page and no named human behind it will struggle to convert. LinguaBoard wins on humanness before it wins on product.

### 4.3 One-Time Payment Is a Market Strategy, Not Just a Pricing Model
Nigerian schools have been burned by software subscriptions — products that raised prices after lock-in, products that went offline when the company folded, products that charged in dollars during naira devaluation crises. The "one-time payment, yours forever" framing is not just financially attractive. It removes a category of fear that is specific to this market and this moment in Nigerian economic history.

The landing page makes this explicit and prominent. It is a market-specific positioning choice, not a generic pricing decision.

### 4.4 Offline Is a Feature, Not a Compromise
In markets with reliable internet, "offline" is a limitation. In Nigerian secondary schools, "offline" is a superpower. NEPA, poor school WiFi infrastructure, and data cost mean that internet-dependent software fails regularly. A product that works without internet does not just solve a convenience problem — it solves a trust problem. The school knows it will work.

The page uses "offline" as a positive, prominent, repeated feature. This is the correct instinct for this market.

## Part 5: What Happens After the Landing Page Works

The landing page is not the end of the prelaunch strategy. It is the beginning of a funnel.

```
Landing page visit
        ↓
Form registration (name + school email)
        ↓
Automated welcome email from Abdullah personally
        ↓
WhatsApp follow-up within 48 hours
        ↓
Short conversation: "What's your biggest challenge with French right now?"
        ↓
School visit / video call — show Figma prototype
        ↓
Deposit commitment
        ↓
Build begins
```

The landing page is step one. Every subsequent step is human. The software does the reach. The founder does the conversion. That is the correct split for a pre-revenue, pre-product stage.

---

## Summary: Three Sentences

The landing page exists because **we are selling before we build** — and selling before building is the only responsible way to start a product that will cost months of work to create.

It is designed for **one specific person in one specific situation** — a Nigerian private school proprietor who has a smartboard and no French teacher — and every word on the page speaks directly to that person and no one else.

It succeeds when **a school owner who had never heard of LinguaBoard fills in a form and then sends the link to another school owner** — because that moment of peer-to-peer referral is worth more than any ad spend and proves that the positioning is true.
